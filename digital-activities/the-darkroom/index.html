The Darkroom

Say what a camera could film

A darkroom-themed simulation where new managers learn to give clear, fair feedback using the SBI method — building feedback as photo layers that develop sharp when they state facts, foggy when they state opinions.

01

Overview

The Darkroom teaches Situation–Behavior–Impact (SBI) feedback. Each part is a colour layer of a photo. Opinions and generalizations make a layer foggy; facts a camera could film make it sharp.

The learner builds feedback, fixes weak feedback, has a branching conversation with a character, and finally writes a real case — watching a photo develop sharp or foggy as they go.

02

Learning Need & Audience

Learning Need
New managers often avoid feedback, or deliver it as a verdict — "you are careless." A verdict starts a defense; a moment, an action, and an effect start a conversation. (An assumption to confirm with a real needs analysis.)
Audience
New managers giving feedback for the first time. About 12–15 minutes, plus a debrief.
Framework
Situation–Behavior–Impact (SBI) feedback.

By the end, a learner can tell what a camera could film from a verdict, build feedback with a situation, a behavior, and an impact, find and replace verdicts in others' feedback, keep a talk on the facts when the other person gets defensive, and write SBI feedback for a real case.

03

Instructional Design

One simple rule: the camera test

If a camera couldn't film it, it's a verdict. The same rule runs through every round, so the idea transfers.

Worked example, then practice

Learners watch a photo develop part by part, take a six-item camera test, and only then build their own.

Four tasks on one skill

Build, spot-and-replace, apply in conversation, and create for real work — different retrieval tasks that reduce rote memorizing.

Named mistakes, then retry

Every wrong card is tagged — verdict, generalization, guess, vague — with a reason. Correct parts stay sharp; only foggy parts are redone.

Round 4 has no score on purpose — the final case is about creating real feedback, not chasing points. Card lengths were balanced so the correct answer is never the longest or shortest by habit.

05

Tools & Honest Limits

Built with HTML, CSS, JavaScript, inline SVG with blend modes and filters, Web Audio, and canvas particles. Accessible by intent: keyboard use, screen-reader labels, reduced-motion support, optional sound, and meaning that never depends on colour alone. Full English and Arabic (RTL), light and dark mode.

SBI Feedback Scenario-Based Learning Retrieval Practice Branching Conversation Bilingual (EN/AR) Accessibility

Honest limits. Scenarios are fictional and not tested with real learners. The Round 4 checker reads words only, so it can miss a verdict or flag fine words. SBI terms should be checked against licensed material. Next: a pilot with 5–10 new managers to measure feedback quality before and after.
