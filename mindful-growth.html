01

Context

Youth workers across Europe are burning out — capable, committed people whose effectiveness is quietly eroded by overload and thin support. Seven organisations, from Latvia to Georgia, had all spotted the same pattern in their teams.

The tricky part: self-care is an easy topic to fill with platitudes, and this audience runs wellbeing sessions for a living. They'd see through anything hollow in minutes.

I had to design something practical enough to convince sceptical professionals, transferable across seven cultures, and personal enough to change habits — in nine days, mostly without slides.

Mindful Growth was the answer, and it was approved for €40,386 in Erasmus+ funding.

03

Process

The whole design sits on Kolb's experiential cycle: an experience, structured reflection, a concept to name it, then a chance to try again. Every session runs that loop at least once — so an activity becomes a transferable insight, not just a well-spent hour.

It leans on behaviour-change research — Fogg and Clear on small, consistent habits — with reflective journaling as the spine. The bet: lasting change comes from tiny repeated actions and honest reflection, not inspiration that fades by Tuesday.

04

The Programme

Nine days built as a journey, not a syllabus — from understanding yourself early, toward practice and peer exchange later. Each day pairs a focus skill with an emotional-awareness thread and closes with reflection.

Days 1–3

Arrive & Notice

Journaling, group agreements, self-observation — participants surface their own patterns before any solution appears.

→
Days 4–6

Practise the Tools

Hands-on experiments in focus, habit-building, and planning — each followed by structured reflection.

→
Days 7–9

Make It Transferable

Peer exchange across countries and a personal follow-up plan: one concrete tool each participant takes home.

Reflection runs at three scales — a daily written pause, a debrief after each activity, and a running journal with Youthpass-aligned self-assessment across the nine days.

05

Key Decisions

The instructional choices that shaped the programme:

Experiential, not a lecture series

Talks would've been faster to build and a guaranteed failure with this audience. Lecture content doesn't transfer across cultures or survive a busy week back home.

Observable objectives only

I refused to write a single objective I couldn't picture someone doing — then defended every activity against that standard.

Reflection as the mechanism

Not a closing courtesy. The experience is raw material; reflection is what turns it into a behaviour someone keeps.

Built for adaptation

Across seven contexts, the goal wasn't a fixed programme but tools flexible enough to survive translation into each facilitator's own room.
