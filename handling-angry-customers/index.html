Handling Angry Customers

Your Shift — a de-escalation simulation

A scenario-based simulation where new support agents work a shift, handle a frustrated customer across chat and phone, and see how their choices change the outcome — before they ever go live.

01

Context

An angry customer is the moment a new support agent is really tested — and the moment they most often get wrong. Reading a policy on de-escalation is nothing like staying calm while someone is furious with you in a live chat.

The gap here is behavioural, not informational. Under pressure, new agents react instead of respond: they defend, skip the acknowledgement, or break process. You can't fix that with a knowledge check — it has to be rehearsed.

So I built a shift they could practise on: a simulation where the customer feels real, but getting it wrong costs nothing but a restart.

02

Learning Need & Objectives

Learning Need
New agents know the de-escalation policy but react instead of respond the first time a customer is genuinely angry.
Target Audience
New customer-service agents, before they take live chat or phone contacts.
  • Acknowledge a customer's emotion before moving to the solution.
  • Choose language that de-escalates rather than defends.
  • Adapt tone and approach across chat and phone.

05

Key Decisions

The instructional choices that shaped the experience:

A simulation, not a quiz

De-escalation is a behaviour, so it has to be rehearsed. A quiz tests recall; a simulation builds the response.

Chat and phone, like the job

Agents switch channels in real work, so the shift does too — a skill practised in context transfers to that context.

A customer with a name

Responding to a person the learner comes to know is what gives the practice its stakes, safely.

Guidance, not hard failure

Supportive nudges keep the learner moving instead of punishing a wrong turn. Safety is what keeps people practising.

Reflection in the flow

Short prompts turn a single choice into a principle the agent can name and carry to the next contact.

Accessible by default

Text-to-speech, keyboard control, and clear feedback mean every agent can finish the shift.

06

The Solution

A branching scenario built as a realistic shift. The agent moves through connected acts, each raising the stakes, and the story shifts with every choice — ending differently depending on how the conversation was handled.

The workplace is rebuilt around them so the practice transfers straight to the job: a live chat window where wording and timing change the customer's response, a phone channel that moves them from typing to speaking, and customer context on screen — the same information a real agent works from.

07

Outcome & Tools

The result is a complete, SCORM-ready simulation an organisation can drop into its existing onboarding — giving new agents a safe place to practise the hardest part of the job before it counts.

Scenario-based learning Branching logic Storyboarding Interaction design Figma SCORM · LMS-ready

What it reinforced. Safety and realism have to be designed together. The scenario only works because the customer feels real — and it only teaches because getting it wrong costs nothing but a restart.
